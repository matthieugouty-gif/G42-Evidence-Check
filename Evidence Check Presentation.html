The problem

Building a committee memo takes too long, and a system that decides needs an auditable path

Slow and manual

For every figure, analysts check several sources, reconcile different definitions by hand, and write down why they chose one. They repeat it for every figure in every memo.

The system can do the legwork

It can look for the best data and choose the best one, so analysts no longer do it by hand. That is what makes memos faster.

But every decision needs an auditable path

What it looked at, what it found, which rule it applied, and who reviewed it, so committees and auditors can replay and challenge it.

Faster memos only help if every decision the system makes can be audited.

The problem is speed: building a memo is slow because analysts check and reconcile every figure by hand. Letting the system decide makes it faster, which raises the next requirement: every decision must be auditable. The EBITDA example comes two slides later, as an illustration.
Where it fits

Evidence Check is the trust layer between the platform's data and the memo

The platform already has the building blocks. Evidence Check turns them into three jobs a committee can see and control.

Router

Decides where to look, and what to skip. Builds on adaptive source routing.

Resolver

Decides which figure to use, and why. Builds on conflict resolution.

Audit trail

Records the full path, down to the source page. Builds on data provenance.

Feedback loops

Learns from what users accept, change and report.

Users see it as Evidence Check inside (In)Alpha and Credit Companion. In the platform it is the Evidence Layer: a source router, a resolver and an audit trail.

This slide places the feature. Evidence Check is not a separate app. It turns three platform capabilities into three jobs: the router decides where to look, the resolver decides which figure to use, and the audit trail records the path. Feedback loops let the engine improve. Mention the Evidence Layer name once, here.
How it decides

Two engines decide: one chooses where to look, the other chooses which figure

Router: where should we look?

  • Stored or borrower documents first
  • The lower-cost vendor next
  • A live lookup only when it must be live
  • Every skipped source is recorded with its reason

You control: edit, reorder, switch off or add rules.

Resolver: which figure, and why?

  • Trust order: the loan agreement's own definition, then audited filings, then management and vendors
  • Two kinds of decision: pick one, or label both bases
  • Never an average
  • Always a plain reason, with the effect of each alternative

You control: accept, change it with a reason, or save your own rule.

Both engines write every step to the audit trail.

This is the logic the demo will show. The router answers where to look and skips what cannot help. The resolver answers which figure and why, with two kinds of decision. The client can change the router's rules and override the resolver's decision. Everything is logged.

The learning loop

The product team sees what the client does not, and uses it to improve

The client sees

  • The decision, the reason and the evidence
  • Which sources were used or skipped
  • The audit trail of every step

No vendor costs, no response times.

The product team sees, across many memos

  • Which sources change the answer, at what cost and speed
  • How often users accept or override a decision, and why
  • Ratings and reported problems
  • A backlog of improvements to the router and the resolver

Why Investment AI, and what success looks like

What is commodity, what is Investment AI's, and how we will know it works

Commodity: reuse

  • Citations and document chat
  • Semantic search
  • Basic extraction from filings

Investment AI's own: build

  • A router that explains what it skipped and why
  • Two kinds of decision: pick one, label both
  • Client rules learned from committee decisions
  • Sovereign and Arabic-native workflows

Success looks like

  • Any figure traceable to its source page in two clicks
  • No memo reaches committee with a figure nobody has reviewed
  • Every decision can be replayed
  • We know which sources are worth paying for

Baselines in the first 30 days, then targets.

Be explicit about what you would not build. Citations are table stakes. The differentiation is the router that explains itself, two kinds of decision, client rule memory and source-value measurement, on a sovereign and Arabic-native stack.
