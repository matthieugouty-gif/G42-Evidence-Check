Evidence Check: prove every number in an investment decision

A capability of the Financial Data Intelligence Platform, built into (In)Alpha and Credit Companion.

Open with the promise: every number in a committee memo can be traced, explained and challenged. Then go straight to the problem.
The problem

The numbers in a memo disagree, and nobody can see why one was chosen

Project Falcon, a $485.0M loan to a private borrower. The covenant limit is 3.50x leverage, tested on the loan agreement's own definition of EBITDA.

Analysts reconcile these by hand. The committee cannot see why one was chosen, or challenge it.

Illustrative demo data
Same company, three EBITDAs, each defensible. The covenant is tested on the contract definition, so the choice moves headroom between 0.20x and 0.48x.
The insight

Nobody is wrong: the figures answer different questions

The gap is explainable

  • $142.0M audited operating EBITDA
  • + $8.5M share-based pay, allowed by the loan agreement: $150.5M
  • + $4.5M lease adjustment, not allowed: management's $155.0M

Where to look is a question of value

  • A private borrower has no vendor data, so asking vendors wastes time and money
  • A listed target needs one live lookup, not five

So the product does two jobs: decide where to look, and decide which figure to use, and why.

What happens before the result

  • The request arrives with its context: memo type, persona, workflow, client, constraints
  • Documents are read and each figure is pinned to entity, period, unit and definition
  • The router plans where to look and records what it skipped
  • Figures are extracted, compared, and the gap explained
  • The trust rule is applied: the engine decides and explains why

Backup · Oversight

The engine decides by default. You stay in control.

Settled

Sources agree

Verified. Nothing to do.

Decided by the system

Every conflict

The system always decides and explains why. You review it, accept it, or change it with a reason. Examples: Adjusted EBITDA is the loan agreement definition ($150.5M); the multiple is 12.4x LTM and 12.8x NTM, both labelled.

Optional: sign-off

Set by client policy

A client can require a named person to sign off specific figures, for example those that drive a covenant or a rating. Off by default: the system still decides first.

An autonomy dial, per client and workflow

The default is that the system decides and you review. A client can tighten it for specific figures, and the platform recommends when to tighten or loosen it from the override rates.

Accountability stays visible

The audit trail records who decided: the system or a person, the rule applied, and any override with its reason. Overrides teach the platform.

In both cases the system always decides and explains why. Named sign-off is an optional client policy.
